Evidence when it helps, uncertainty when it matters

The experience brings bounded appliance evidence into service work without turning a symptom into a diagnosis or a card into an autonomous decision maker.

Design principles

Trust comes from restraint and clarity

Each principle is an operational choice, not decoration. Together they keep evidence useful without overstating what it can prove.

01

Evidence before interpretation

Show what was observed, when, and from which approved source before limited explanation.

02

One contract, host-aware

Keep evidence meaning consistent while respecting case work and conversational work.

03

Progressive disclosure

Profile first; connected evidence, History, and Assessment only when requested and allowed.

04

Unknown is valid

Missing, stale, ambiguous, denied, and unavailable are explicit states.

05

Human-controlled next step

Offer one validation question, never an automatic repair or service action.

06

Quiet operational UI

Optimize for scanning, comparison, and repeated service work instead of spectacle.

Users and jobs

One experience, different decisions

The service user needs immediate context; technical owners need confidence in the boundaries behind it.

SERVICE REPRESENTATIVE

Understand the symptom in context

Review appliance identity, evidence mode, freshness, unknowns, and one next question without leaving the case.

SERVICE SPECIALIST

Inspect bounded depth

Review a fixed event window and distinguish observations from possible interpretation.

SERVICE LEADER

Evaluate the operating pattern

Confirm consistent controls and measurable states before endorsing a broader proof.

Representative journey

Seven deliberate steps from symptom to decision

The experience narrows the next validation step. It does not skip the qualified person who owns the service decision.

  1. Hear the symptomA customer reports that the dishwasher is not draining.
  2. Resolve contextFind one approved appliance or show ambiguity.
  3. Orient with profileShow identity before connected evidence.
  4. Request evidenceLoad approved observations and freshness.
  5. Inspect depthOpen History or Assessment on demand.
  6. Keep unknownsSeparate evidence from interpretation.
  7. Choose the stepA qualified person decides what happens next.

Surface roles

The host changes; the evidence boundary does not

Each surface has one clear job, which prevents duplicate workflows and inconsistent evidence meaning.

Information model

Overview first, depth only when useful

The three views reflect a progressive service conversation rather than three equal tabs loaded in advance.

Overview

Which appliance? Which mode? What was observed? How fresh? What is unknown? What should be validated next?

  • Always available after authorized resolution
  • Useful in profile-only mode

History

Approved events or measurements in a bounded, source-aware window.

  • On demand
  • Unavailable in profile-only mode

Assessment

Observations, limited deterministic interpretation, unknowns, and one next validation question.

  • No root-cause claim
  • No repair prescription

Responsive and accessible

Stable across hosts, sizes, and input methods

The design must remain coherent in a narrow side pane, a conversational card, a full browser, print, light theme, and dark theme.

  • Visible keyboard focus and logical reading order
  • Semantic landmarks and heading hierarchy
  • State never communicated by color alone
  • No horizontal page scrolling on phone, tablet, or desktop
  • Stable dimensions during loading and mode changes
  • Mobile prioritizes identity, mode, freshness, and next question
  • Light and dark themes preserve contrast
  • Reduced motion removes nonessential movement
  • Zoom and text reflow preserve controls and meaning
  • Print/save retains evidence boundary and source context

Decisions and tradeoffs

What the design intentionally does not optimize for

The pattern chooses trust, consistency, and bounded depth over broad automation and simplified certainty.

Profile first

More useful than a blocked screen; less risk than silently loading diagnostics.

Depth on demand

Reduces unnecessary access and first-view density, with an extra explicit step.

One card across hosts

Improves semantic consistency; requires careful host context and sizing adapters.

Deterministic explanation

Improves repeatability and safety while intentionally limiting speculative guidance.

Read-only proof

Builds trust before action automation; case and appliance actions remain outside scope.

Visible uncertainty

Requires more careful reading but prevents a false health or diagnosis signal.

Current and target

Validate the pattern before designing production

The current proof confirms interaction mechanics. The customer target requires discovery with real roles, journeys, sources, content, brand, accessibility, and measures.

Current proof

Demo personas, five synthetic Cove scenarios, verified Dynamics/Cowork pattern, customer-safe neutral package, and technical interaction checks.

Customer-owned target

Confirmed roles, ranked journeys, customer field semantics, approved host placement, brand and content guidance, assistive-technology testing, and agreed usability/service measures.

Open design questions

What discovery must decide

These questions stay visible because answering them changes the experience materially.

  • Which roles receive profile-only versus connected access?
  • Which evidence belongs in Overview, History, and Assessment?
  • What counts as fresh, stale, missing, ambiguous, and unavailable?
  • What terminology, localization, units, and date/time conventions apply?
  • How should density differ between case and conversational hosts?
  • Which assistive-technology tests and accessibility requirements apply?
  • What approved brand and content guidance should replace the neutral demo treatment?
  • What escalation, Customer Care, certified-service, and fallback language is right?
  • Which usability, service, and evidence-quality measures define success?
